How to read this:
- **Proven** = established by exhaustive search or by replaying a witness through the game rules. **Designer estimate** = my judgement, provisional. **Human-tested** = nothing yet.
- *Witness replay*: the recorded solution, replayed through `PuzzleSession`, is legal at every step, respects the bell budget and the order sequence, and ends in a win.
- *Min rings*: the smallest bell budget with a solution; every smaller budget was searched exhaustively and found UNSOLVABLE.
- *Choice points*: steps of the witness where at least one available action is a proven dead end (exhaustive search from the resulting position) and at least one isn't. *Non-bell* excludes dead ends that are only "ring a bell now" (with a budget of 1, spending the bell early is a dead end at many steps, which inflates the raw count).
- *First actions*: how many of the opening actions keep the page solvable, with the page's constraints and with budget/sequence removed.
- Solver nodes and time are engineering metrics, **not** human difficulty.
- A move = one crate tap or one bell ring. The precision target is offered only when it is proven minimal: every solution taps once per item, and fewer rings than the proven minimum are impossible.
